Build your button first; I'd call it **Play Along**. It's cheap because the engine already does the hard part. Then grow it into the 10x feature, **Conductor**: lights that know the song, not just the beat.

I based this on four parallel studies (the music and Composer code, every other screen on `main`, the competitor market, and the new Apple, Hue and music-analysis technology as of this month). I re-checked the key code claims myself.

**The big finding:** ChromaGlow already has the hard parts. It has a beat clock, 25 fps streaming with real lamp positions, a layered Composer with per-layer beat lock and sound settings, song recognition through Shazam and Apple Music, and, on your Spotify path, audio decoded 1.2–3.2 s before anyone hears it. None of this reaches the person holding the phone. Most of what follows is connecting those parts, plus one new piece that understands song structure.

Sizes below: S = days, M = 1–2 weeks, L = several weeks.

## Your button: Play Along
Today only 2 of the 49 looks react to sound (Beat Drop and Club Pulse; not even Disco Fever or Neon Nights). Making any other look react takes about 8 taps through Layers → Edit → Sound for a single layer, and the Tune page has no sound control. The engine already supports all of it per layer, including a Beat lock setting (1/2/4/8 beats) hidden in the Rhythm tab.

Play Along is one ♪ button next to Go Live and on Home's Now Playing card. It plays a musical version of the look without changing the saved look:
- **It syncs like a DJ's Sync button.** Every layer's cycle snaps to the nearest musical length and locks to the bar. Aurora Drift's 14-second breathe becomes 8 bars at 120 BPM. The look keeps its character and just moves in time.
- **Each layer gets a musical role.** The base layer swells with loudness, accent layers change colour on the beat, and lightning, sparkles and fireworks fire on drum hits instead of random timers.
- **One Feel dial:** Glide (timing only) → Groove (adds swells) → Hit (adds accents). It always stays under the existing limit of 3 flashes a second.
- **It finds the music itself:** Apple Music if it's playing, otherwise the mic plus Shazam.

Three engine gaps need closing: Composer 2 motion has no beat lock, Beat lock doesn't start listening on its own, and a low-confidence tempo guess can lock in for a whole song. **S–M.** *"Lava Lamp still looks like Lava Lamp. It's just dancing."*

## The 10x: Conductor. Your lights stop reacting to music and start performing it
*You put on a song. The intro glows, the verse breathes, and the lights tighten and climb through the build. The room goes dark half a beat before the drop, then everything hits on the one and sweeps out from the speaker. The chorus takes the album's colours. You touched nothing.*

**Why it's 10x.** Every app does "beat → flash", which is why music sync looks random after 30 seconds. A lighting designer thinks in three timescales: the beat, the phrase and the whole song. Conductor adds the last two.
- The only consumer product that ever knew song structure was Hue × Spotify, and Spotify shut that data off for new apps in Nov 2024.
- Specialist apps each have one piece: iLightShow pre-analyses tracks, SonicHue advertises "Predictive Drop", SoundSwitch writes per-track scripts for DJs.
- None combines any music source, song structure, a real look engine and one button. Hue's own app still has no phone-mic sync at all.

**How it knows what's coming.** Three levels sit behind the same button:
1. **Knows.** When ChromaGlow plays the audio itself (imported files, and your personal Spotify build), it analyses the sound 1.2 s (phone speaker) to 3.2 s (AirPlay) before it's heard. Those results already sit in a buffer, and nothing reads ahead into them today. Reading them lets the lights see the drop coming. Apple's new Music Understanding framework (iOS 27) adds beats, bars, sections, key and per-instrument activity for a whole file.
2. **Listens.** For everything else (Apple Music, the Spotify app, vinyl, a live band), run Music Understanding on rolling chunks of mic audio. On a Mac each 4–15 s chunk took about 0.15 s; iPhone speed is unmeasured. It counts 8-bar phrases, spots builds (rising drums, pace and loudness), and stages the drop for the next phrase boundary. If the drop doesn't come, the lights ease back so the miss looks intentional.
3. **Remembers.** It saves each song's map (analysis only, never audio) under the song's ID from Shazam or Apple Music. The second time the song plays, its playback position plus the saved map gives full look-ahead, even on Apple Music. *"The second time I played it, the lights knew the song."*

**What you see.** A thin song timeline on the stage: sections as coloured blocks, a playhead, and a "drop in 4… 3…" pulse. Tap a section to pin a different look. That becomes your **Song Show** for that track: it replays next time, and you can share it as a link that adapts to the other person's room. It's your existing Sequence Builder design, timed to the music.

**Build order:** Play Along → reading ahead on audio ChromaGlow plays → saved song maps → phrase prediction from the mic → timeline editing and sharing.

## 10 Composer and music-sync upgrades
1. **On the One.** *"Everything I tap lands on the beat."* While music plays, look changes, Tune drags and colour swaps wait for the next bar. Between songs, colours crossfade across the transition, like Apple Music's AutoMix. This needs real bar detection: today "beat 1" is just whichever beat locked first. **S**
2. **Sync Check.** *"Five seconds, and 'close' became 'tight'."* Point the phone at a lamp near the speaker. It plays four clicks and flashes the lamp, hears one and sees the other, and measures the true delay for AirPlay, Bluetooth or the phone speaker. Lights then fire early by that amount. Today nothing compensates for bridge or bulb delay, and no competitor I found calibrates automatically. It also answers your open question: does the 3.2 s AirPlay wait actually land on the beat? **M**
3. **Every song paints the room.** *"Each song repainted the room in its cover art."* On a track change, the look takes the album's colours, crossfaded on the first downbeat. The colour extractor is already built, but only Studio Classic's old looks can use it; in the Composer tab and on Home the album thumbnail is only a preview. **S**
4. **The room is the band.** *"I could see the bass line walking along the floor."* Bass plays in the floor lamps, vocals at eye height, and drums and cymbals on the ceiling and lightstrips. Entertainment Areas already store each lamp's height, but music can't drive position today. Music Understanding reports vocal, drum and bass activity. **M**
5. **The whole house joins.** *"The chorus hit and every room changed."* An Entertainment Area is capped at 10 lights and one stream per bridge, even on Bridge Pro. So the area plays the beat at 25 fps, while every other room changes on the bar and the section using the slower room-by-room mode. One reviewer with 80 lights wrote "this app does absolutely nothing." **M**
6. **Party Remote.** *"Everyone was playing the lights from their own phone, and nobody installed anything."* Show a QR code, and guests open a small web page that your iPhone serves over Wi-Fi. They can vote on the next vibe, send a colour, or hit Drop. Every hit lands on the beat and respects the flash limit. Guests never touch the bridge or get a key. (App Clips can't use the local network, which is why it's a web page.) Nobody offers this today. **M**
7. **Perform in the Composer.** *"I double-tapped my fingers and the room hit on the one."* Bring Perform's pads, which today live only in Studio Classic and only work with old looks, to every Composer look. Add Apple Watch double tap as a hit, Tap Dial buttons as pads, and a 2-bar looper. Taps that are slightly late snap onto the beat. **M**
8. **Pocket mode.** *"I locked my phone and the party kept going."* "The phone must stay awake" is the #4 complaint in competitor reviews. When ChromaGlow plays the audio itself, background audio keeps the show alive. With the mic, offer an honest "keep listening" mode that shows the system's mic indicator. Otherwise, hand the bridge a dynamic scene at the song's tempo so the room never freezes. Add a Live Activity with the song, the look and Stop. It can't pulse with the beat, because Live Activities don't update in real time. **M**
9. **Capture a Vibe.** *"I filmed the campfire and my living room became the campfire."* Film five seconds of a fire, waves, a neon sign or club lights. ChromaGlow measures the colours, motion and flicker rate and builds a living look that moves the same way. Hue and Govee make looks from text; nobody makes them from real-world motion. **M**
10. **Breed looks.** *"Thunderstorm × Lava Lamp made something I'd never have built."* Drag one look onto another. The new look takes one parent's colours and the other's motion, plus a random twist. **S**

## 10 upgrades for the rest of the app
1. **Scan Your Room.** *"It blinked each bulb and drew my room."* Sweep the camera while each lamp blinks, and the phone places it in 3D. Real positions replace "Positions: estimated" in every look, and a correct Entertainment Area is created without a trip to the Hue app. Hue's own SpatialAware does a version of this, but only on Bridge Pro and only for scenes. **M–L**
2. **Automations that actually run.** *"My sunrise worked while my phone was dead in a drawer."* Today a schedule "runs when you tap its reminder, or right away if the app is open." Move schedules onto the bridge: the code to create bridge schedules and rules already exists, though only stored animations use it today. Hue's built-in Wake up and Go to sleep routines also appear to be creatable by apps (documented by the community, not officially; verify on your bridge). **M**
3. **It notices.** *"It spotted my routine before I did."* After a week of watching what happens, it asks: "You turn on Relax in the Living Room around 9:40 on weeknights. Make it automatic?" One tap creates it. The app already has an unused database table for this activity log. Hue's new AI automations make you type a description; this asks for nothing. **M**
4. **Shake to undo the house.** *"The kids wrecked the lights. One shake and everything was back."* Keep a short history of every change and restore the whole house to how it was 10 minutes ago, or to how it was before the party. The Composer already does this for one room. **M**
5. **Lights that hear the house.** *"With headphones on, the lights told me someone was at the door."* A free "Alert flash" action with a different pattern for doorbell, smoke alarm and baby crying, hooked to iOS Sound Recognition automations, which run without asking. Hue charges €3.99 a month for smoke-alarm light alerts. It needs Apple's "don't rely on this in an emergency" warning. **S**
6. **Glow to someone.** *"Mom's lamp turned pink while I was 1,000 miles away."* Tap a person, and their lamp, in their home, glows your colour for a moment. Only the glow travels between homes (through Apple's iCloud); no bridge keys ever leave anyone's home. This needs a security review. **M–L**
7. **Give your old iPhone a job.** *"My drawer phone became a Sync Box."* Plug in a spare iPhone or iPad near the TV and it becomes the always-on brain: music shows without your phone, TV sync through its camera (Hue sells a $119.99 camera for this), sound alerts and schedules. **M**
8. **Night path.** *"3 a.m. bathroom trip, and nobody got blinded."* At night, motion lights a 10% amber path and then fades. Today the app ignores motion, temperature and light-level sensors. Bridge Pro also lets regular bulbs act as motion sensors. **M**
9. **Light my room like this.** *"I screenshotted a movie still and my room became it."* From any photo, screenshot or iOS Visual Intelligence result, the colours land where they sit in the picture: the left of the sunset goes to the left of the room. **S–M**
10. **Share anything as a link.** *"A friend sent a look and it was in my room in one tap."* Composer looks, Song Shows and Hue scenes become links that preview as living cards. Today only Studio Classic looks can be shared. **S–M**

## Reality checks
- **Your Spotify receiver can't ship to the public.** Spotify's developer policy bans syncing recordings with visual media and analysing its content. Spotify also warned the open-source project your receiver is built on (Nov 2025), and its copy protection now breaks on newer accounts. Keep it as personal R&D for the "Knows" level; the public path is the mic, Apple Music, Shazam and Music Understanding.
- **Music Understanding can't read Apple Music** (copy protection) or whatever the system is playing, and on live audio it only reports loudness. That's why Conductor needs the Listens and Remembers levels.
- **Your closest rivals already ship pieces of this.** hueDynamic has beat-snapped pads and camera TV sync; Lumina has a room map, album colours and a Live Activity. Your edge is the Composer engine plus song structure plus zero setup.

## Bugs I found while digging (from reading code, not reproduced on a phone)
- **All Day Scenes turns rooms back on.** Every 5 minutes it switches on every room that isn't playing a look, including rooms you turned off (`HueHome/Core/Network/UnifiedOrchestrator.swift:613`). It also only runs while the app is open, yet the Welcome Tour says it works "without you touching a thing" (`HueHome/Core/Models/TutorialCatalog.swift:157`).
- **A low-confidence tempo guess can lock in for a whole track.** The tempo code accepts any BPM regardless of confidence (`HueHome/Core/Audio/BeatClock.swift:275`).
- **Mic analysis runs about 100 ms late.** It reads audio in ~100 ms chunks, and after a song change it needs about 25 s to find the new tempo.
- **While Spotify plays, the Composer's music strip says "Nothing playing",** and Spotify songs get no album colours or tempo lookup.
- **Siri's "start a composition" only lists Studio Classic looks** (`HueHome/Intents/StudioIntents.swift:52`).

## What I'd build first
1. Play Along, On the One, Every song paints the room, and the tempo and beat-lock fixes. They're small and change how the app feels within weeks.
2. Sync Check, Party Remote, and Perform in the Composer.
3. Conductor, in the order above.
4. Alongside those: Automations that actually run, then It notices, then Scan Your Room.
