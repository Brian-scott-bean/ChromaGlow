Groove map — build 916 (2026-11-02). Do drum looks light ON the drum?

run.sh -groove -n 60: every n-th Groove MIDI take (60) and every BabySlakh mix (20), line and a measured home-like
room. The whole live chain runs causally: OnsetFrameAnalyzer (+ DrumLanes), the BeatTracker → BeatClock pass every
0.5 s, the GrooveMap fed from the same frames on the clock's grid; lights rendered every 20 ms for when they are SHOWN
(render lead 0.1 s). A light hit "starts" on the first rendered frame that carries it.

  heard   = build 915: what the drum lanes hear (reactive).
  groove  = build 916: the same, plus the groove map's predicted hits (GroovePattern.latestHit).
  on-time = a light hit within ±50 ms of an annotated stroke (≥ velocity 40); P against every stroke of the drum.
  median error = over strokes with a light hit within ±150 ms. any-time P = light hits within −10…+150 ms of a
  played stroke (late or not). per stroke = light hits per annotated stroke.

What it shows: reactive drum hits land ~+135 ms late (the 0.1 s lead plus the analysis hop), so almost none are on
time. Predicted hits land on the stroke (median +16…+24 ms on the mixes) wherever the clock is trusted and the
pattern repeats — 59 % of frames on line mixes, 27–44 % on the human Groove MIDI takes. The cost: predicted slots
the drummer didn't play this bar are extra (softer — certainty-scaled) flashes: light hits per kick 1.20 → 1.78 on
line mixes, and any-time precision 0.57 → 0.47. A sweep (-sweep: stricter slot weight, agreement, faster
suspension, wider merge window) traded on-time hits for fewer extras at a worse F everywhere, and the merge window
changed nothing (the extras are not double fires); the standard tuning stays.
